In August, CARE went live in Bhutan as the national home care grid. During the same month, the OHC team travelled to Bhutan to discuss and demonstrate CARE in palliative care settings, and to explore how the two sides could work together on palliative solutions.

## A national home care grid

Bhutan has deployed CARE as its national home care grid, bringing care delivered in people's homes onto one shared platform. It also takes CARE beyond India, showing that an open, standards based platform built for Indian public health can be adopted by another country's health system.

## The first DPG under the GNH protocol

CARE will be the first digital public good under Bhutan's Gross National Happiness (GNH) protocol. Gross National Happiness is the framework Bhutan uses to guide its national policy, and CARE's place within it reflects a fit between an openly governed platform and a health system built around the wellbeing of its people.

## A visit focused on palliative care

During the visit, the team gave several demonstrations of CARE, each built around how the platform works inside palliative setups. The sessions were a chance to show the workflows in practice and to understand what palliative care teams in Bhutan would need from the system.

## Built on Kerala's experience

CARE has supported home based palliative care at scale in Kerala through the Kerala Palliative Care Grid, which launched statewide in March 2025. The Grid supports field teams on home visits and gives health officials a clear view of who is being cared for and how. That experience shaped both the demonstrations and the national grid in Bhutan.
